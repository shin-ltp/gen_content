The U.S. CDC and the FDA said on Wednesday they are investigating ​a multistate Salmonella outbreak linked to ‌jalapeno peppers from Mexico, which have sickened 345 people across 27 states and prompted a recall of affected products.

The FDA said 36 ​hospitalizations and no deaths have been ​reported, while the investigation remains ongoing.

The health agencies linked ⁠the outbreak to jalapeno peppers supplied by ​a common grower in Sinaloa and distributed ​in the U.S. by Coast Citrus Distributors.

The company has agreed to recall the remaining product and is ​notifying affected customers, the FDA said.

The U.S. investigation ​identified restaurants including Chipotle Mexican Grill (CMG.N), opens new tab and QDOBA, a ‌Mexican-style ⁠restaurant chain, as recipients of jalapenos imported by Coast Citrus Distributors.

Chipotle has since switched jalapeno suppliers for its restaurants beginning July ​20, while ​QDOBA stopped ⁠using jalapenos on July 28 and is no longer serving them, ​according to the FDA.

&quot;FDA does not ​consider ⁠there to be a current ongoing risk from these establishments to consumers in this ⁠outbreak,&quot; the ​Food and Drug Administration said in ​a statement.
