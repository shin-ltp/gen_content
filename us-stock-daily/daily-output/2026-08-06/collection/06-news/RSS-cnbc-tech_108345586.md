CNBC&#x27;s Jim Cramer said Wednesday investors looking at Elon Musk&#x27;s SpaceX should stop thinking in quarters and start thinking in decades .

&quot;SpaceX could be a 100-year piece of paper,&quot; the &quot; Mad Money &quot; host said, arguing that SpaceX should be viewed much like the century-long railroad bonds that ultimately rewarded patient investors. &quot;Maybe you put some away for the next generation or even the one after that.&quot;

Shares of SpaceX fell 13.6% on Wednesday as Wall Street digested the rocket-and-AI company&#x27;s first earnings report since its historic June IPO. While revenue in the quarter topped expectations, the company disclosed sharply higher capital expenditures.

Cramer acknowledged the stock could face additional near-term pressure with roughly 911 million previously locked-up shares becoming eligible for trading Thursday.

Still, he argued investors focused on the next few quarters are missing the bigger picture. Instead, he thinks SpaceX&#x27;s long-term value rests on Musk&#x27;s ability to repeatedly build transformative businesses.

While SpaceX&#x27;s ambitions will require enormous amounts of capital, Cramer said Musk has consistently proven he can raise money for projects many initially dismissed as unrealistic.

&quot;I would never recommend SpaceX if Musk weren&#x27;t involved,&quot; he said. &quot;I&#x27;m confident that Musk can raise all of the money he needs.&quot;

Cramer also pointed to several long-term growth opportunities across the company, including Starship, Musk&#x27;s fully reusable rocket program; Starlink&#x27;s expanding satellite internet business; and SpaceX&#x27;s growing compute footprint, which could eventually be used internally or rented to more outside customers. Currently, SpaceX has compute-rental agreements with Claude creator Anthropic and Alphabet&#x27;s Google.

While Cramer acknowledged those businesses may take years — or even decades — to reach their full potential, he said that&#x27;s precisely why investors should evaluate SpaceX differently from a typical stock.

&quot;One day this stock could be a huge winner,&quot; he said. &quot;I just don&#x27;t know when that day will come.&quot;
