This is CNBC&#x27;s live coverage of SpaceX&#x27;s 2026 second-quarter earnings.

SpaceX reported better-than-expected revenue for the second quarter in the company&#x27;s first earnings report since its record IPO in June. The stock dropped about 8% in extended trading as capital expenditures soared.

Here&#x27;s how the company did compared with analysts&#x27; estimates, according to LSEG

Revenue jumped 92% from $4.1 billion a year earlier, SpaceX said in a statement on Tuesday, while the company&#x27;s net loss narrowed to $541 million from $1 billion.

It was the first time for Elon Musk&#x27;s reusable rocket maker to face Wall Street in this capacity, and investors were jittery. Since opening at $150 on June 12, SpaceX&#x27;s stock has dropped by 16% as of Tuesday&#x27;s close.

SpaceX lost $4.9 billion last year, largely due to hefty investments in artificial intelligence infrastructure. The company merged with Musk&#x27;s xAI in February, saying at the time that the vision was to build data centers in space. But even the launch business, which counts on large contracts from the U.S. Defense Department and NASA, is losing money.

Most of SpaceX&#x27;s revenue last year, and its only source of profit, came from its connectivity segment, which consists of its Starlink satellite internet service. Starlink is sold directly to consumers, as well as to government and military agencies.

The operating loss for the the space unit was $542 million, while the AI unit lost $1.26 billion. Connectivity remained profitable, with operating income in the period of $1.66 billion.

CNBC&#x27;s reporters are covering SpaceX earnings from bureaus in San Francisco and Englewood Cliffs, New Jersey.

Shotwell said on the call that during the quarter SpaceX launched Starlink mobile partnerships with international carriers, including SoftBank, NTT DoCoMo and Spark New Zealand.

She said the &quot;near-term priority&quot; is launching the company&#x27;s mobile V2 satellites on Starship before an integration with EchoStar spectrum next year. A spectrum transfer was recently approved by the Federal Communications Commission, and &quot;represents a foundational competitive advantage for Starlink mobile,&quot; Shotwell said.

SpaceX will later go after AT&amp;T , Verizon and T-Mobile customers with its forthcoming Starlink mobile service as early as next year, and plans to build out a terrestrial network in the U.S. using the EchoStar spectrum, Shotwell said.

The company aims to fly satellites that can deliver the standalone service in 2027, she said, and to be able to provide Starlink Mobile to its first customers by the end of next year as well.

&quot;I expect us to be able to acquire quite a few of their customers,&quot; Shotwell said. &quot;I think our service will be better. We will eliminate dead zones, leveraging, basically, satellites in orbit.&quot;

As the earnings call was wrapping up, Musk responded to a question about the Cursor deal by choosing not to respond.

The $60 billion transaction is expected to close in the third quarter.

&quot;We&#x27;re trying to get the acquisition closed as quickly as possible,&quot; Musk said. &quot;I think we&#x27;re pretty close to that. But we&#x27;re wary of sort of jumping the gun on regulatory closures.&quot;

Musk has clashed with regulators in the past over social media posts concerning material information about deals, including his Twitter leveraged buyout, and a take-private deal for Tesla he said was in the works but that never materialized.

Nvidia shares rose about 2% in extended trading after Musk said the chipmaker has the &quot;best AI computer&quot; and pledged that SpaceX would use its Vera Rubin processors exclusively.

Ad revenue in the quarter dropped 14% from a year earlier, a decline SpaceX blamed on a migration to a new advertising system.

In April SpaceX started to release the new technology, including the X Ads Manager, according to its prospectus. SpaceX said the X Ads Manager was meant to help advertisers more quickly launch campaigns, with one system for creation, optimization and monitoring.

Ad sales fell to $367 million from $426 million in the same quarter last year.

Johnsen said that SpaceX&#x27;s capital deployment is so efficient that the company is seeing returns in very short order.

&quot;All capex is not the same,&quot; Johnsen said on the call. &quot;Specifically on the AI compute side, we&#x27;re able to deploy capital in such a way that we&#x27;re getting less than a one-year payback.&quot;

He added that capital is becoming an item that would otherwise be put in cost of goods sold, or COGS, &quot;because it&#x27;s moving so quickly.&quot;

On the earnings call, Musk talked up the potential for SpaceX&#x27;s Starlink satellite internet service to maintain and expand its lead.

&quot;It&#x27;s not out of the question that, at some point, Starlink will deliver a majority of the world&#x27;s internet, at least in countries where we are allowed to operate, which is the vast majority of countries,&quot; Musk said. He added that he thinks this can happen in &quot;less than 10 years.&quot;

SpaceX named more than 20 companies as competitors to Starlink in its prospectus, including Amazon, Blue Origin, Viasat , AT&amp;T and T-Mobile . Some competitors are also customers, using SpaceX launches to get their satellites into orbit.

Shotwell said on the call that she&#x27;s &quot;very bullish&quot; on both enterprise and government growth for Starlink moving forward.

Musk said Starlink had offered only &quot;patchy&quot; connectivity in its early days, but that it has &quot;incredible uptime and low latency&quot; today, so more offices and agencies can treat Starlink as a &quot;primary provider&quot; of internet service, not a backup provider.

SpaceX CFO Bret Johnsen said on the earnings call that the company is on pace to reach $100 billion in annualized recurring revenue by the end of the year.

Johnsen said that in the first few weeks of the current quarter, SpaceX has contracted an additional $6.7 billion of cloud services revenue &quot;over a six month period that begins ramping starting in October.&quot;

The figure for the end of the year assumes contributions from cursor, Johnsen said.

Musk told shareholders in his opening remarks on the earnings call that SpaceX plans to build its AI data centers exclusively using Nvidia chips. He said he expects to end 2026 with over 2 gigawatts of compute, growing closer to 10 gigawatts by the end of 2027.

The AI infrastructure will go to support the company&#x27;s own AI model development and services, and will help cloud computing customers.

Musk said the company sees Nvidia&#x27;s Vera Rubin as &quot;the best AI computer.&quot;

&quot;And we greatly value our close cooperation and partnership on many levels with Nvidia,&quot; he said.

SpaceX announced a partnership with Nvidia to collaborate on the design of &quot;space compute,&quot; or hardware for use running AI models and services on satellites in orbit, powered by solar panels and the sun.
